The Fix: 5 Rules That Align Us With Industry Standards

We researched Clash of Clans, Civilization, Age of Empires, Cookie Clicker, AdVenture Capitalist, and Clicker Heroes. Our Tier 1-3 economy is healthy and follows industry patterns. But the proposed 10-level upgrade numbers, build times, and income model have critical problems. Here's the fix:

The 5 Rules
1. Upgrade multiplierUse 1.12x per level for within-item upgrades (not 2-3x). Industry range: 1.07-1.15.
2. Build time cap14 days max. Clash caps at 14-15 days. Beyond that, players disengage.
3. Income scales with costsEach new building tier must unlock new income. Costs are exponential but income is currently flat — no game survives this.
4. Payoff time ruleEvery purchase should pay for itself in 3-7 days of passive income at that tier.
5. Fix broken items first3 shop items take shards for zero effect. Fix these before adding new content.

Industry Deep Dive: How Clash, Civ, AoE & Idle Games Scale

We researched five proven models. The universal lesson: costs grow exponentially, income grows alongside them, and the multiplier per level decreases at higher tiers.

Clash of Clans — The Gold Standard for Mobile Strategy

LevelCost (Gold)Build TimeMultiplierKey Mechanic
TH21,00010 sec—Tutorial
TH5150,00012 hrs~2-4xUnlocks better troops = more raid income
TH82,000,0002 days~1.3-1.6xArmy costs scale but so does loot
TH127,000,0006 days~1.3xSiege machines unlock massive raid payouts
TH1618,000,00014 days~1.2xMultiplier flattens deliberately
TH1720,000,00015 days1.1xTotal to max: ~8.6B gold, ~3.5 years

Key insights: (1) Multiplier decreases at higher tiers — early is 2-4x, late is 1.1-1.2x. (2) Build times cap at 14-15 days. (3) Supercell actively cut upper-tier costs in Dec 2023 to prevent churn. (4) Income scales up as you upgrade — better troops = better raids = cost-to-earnings ratio stays roughly constant. Costs are hand-tuned per level, not a single formula.

Civilization 6 — Polynomial, Not Exponential

MechanicScaling ModelKey Insight
District costs60 → 600 across tech tree (~10x total). Each tech adds ~15% of base cost.Costs grow with progress, not number of districts
Settlers80, 100, 120, 140, 160... (linear +20 per city)Linear, not exponential — keeps expansion viable
Production (income)Scales via city/district improvements at roughly same rate as costsIncome-cost parity maintained throughout

Key insight: Civ uses polynomial cost scaling, not exponential. This keeps everything reachable. Old Civ games used corruption/happiness to limit expansion; Civ 6 uses cost scaling instead. The lesson: you don't need exponential costs to create challenge.

Age of Empires 2 — The Payoff Time Rule

AoE2's economy is built around return on investment. Each economic upgrade costs X resources but increases gathering speed. The benchmark: an upgrade should pay for itself within 3-5 minutes of gameplay. Examples:

  • Bow Saw (wood upgrade): Costs 150F/100G. With 15 lumberjacks, pays off in ~4 minutes.
  • Horse Collar (farming): Costs 75F/75W. Pays off in ~3 minutes per farm.

Key insight: Don't just set prices by "feel" — calculate the payoff time. If a purchase never pays for itself, players learn to skip it. Our equivalent: every building/upgrade should pay for itself in 3-7 days of passive income at that tier.

Idle Games — The Universal Formula

The Math (from Kongregate's idle game research)
Cost = BaseCost × MultiplierLevel
Income = BaseIncome × Owned × Multipliers

Multiplier sweet spot: 1.07 to 1.15
Cookie Clicker: 1.15  |  Clicker Heroes: 1.07  |  AdVenture Capitalist: 1.07-1.15

Critical rule: Costs grow exponentially, income grows polynomially.
Costs MUST eventually outpace income — this creates natural slowdown.
But income must also grow — flat income + exponential costs = unplayable wall.

Worked example (AdVenture Capitalist): Lemonade Stand base cost = 4, growth rate = 1.07. At 10 owned, next costs 7.87. At 50 owned, next costs 117. Production is 1.67/sec × owned. The gap widens but never hits a wall because each purchase still adds meaningful income. Prestige mechanics reset the curve when it flattens.

The Universal Pattern Across All Games

Every successful economy shares these traits:
  • Building-to-building jumps: 2-3x max between tiers (not 6,250x)
  • Within-building upgrades: 1.07-1.15x per level
  • Build times: Cap at 7-14 days
  • Income scales with costs: New tiers unlock new income so the cost/earnings ratio stays roughly constant
  • Multiplier decreases at endgame: Early 2-4x, mid 1.3x, late 1.1-1.2x
  • Payoff time is calculable: Every purchase earns itself back within a reasonable window

On-Demand Image Generation: The Reveal
"Perhaps the next upgrade icon doesn't happen till a person ACTUALLY makes it or requests it, to make it more of a big reveal and something fun to get people to upgrade their items."
— Andy, February 2026

Instead of pre-generating all 10 levels for every item, we generate each upgrade image on demand when the player actually upgrades. This creates a moment of surprise and delight.

Player clicks
"Upgrade to Lv 5"
→
Shards deducted
Build timer starts
→
Flux Kontext runs
Lv4 image + prompt
→
Image saved
to GCS
→
Build complete
New image revealed!

How It Works

  • Input: Previous level's image URL (already in GCS)
  • Prompt: "Upgrade this item: add more Martian red rock, more Sepolia crystal accents, make it bigger and more powerful. Replace any remaining Earth materials with carved stone and regolith. Keep the same cartoon style."
  • Output: New image stored at upgrades/{category}_{item}_lv{N}_{user_id}_{timestamp}.png
  • Timing: Generation happens during the build timer (player doesn't wait extra)
  • Fallback: If Flux fails, show a "Constructing..." shimmer placeholder, retry in background
  • Unique per player: Your Lv7 rover looks slightly different from mine — because Kontext is non-deterministic
Why This Is Better Than Pre-Generated:
  • Every upgrade is a surprise reveal — you don't know what it looks like until you earn it
  • No spoilers — can't preview Lv10 items before getting there
  • Each player's items are slightly unique (Kontext variation)
  • Zero upfront image generation cost — only pay when players actually upgrade
  • Natural progression — each image builds on the previous one visually

Open Question: Merge Shop + Upgrade Systems?

Currently there are TWO systems for similar items:

  • SHOP_CATALOG — 4 separate rover items (basic, enhanced, advanced, elite) bought individually
  • UPGRADE_CATALOG — Rover with 4 levels, upgraded in-place

These overlap. Should we merge into ONE system where rover is just "Rover Lv1-10" with upgrade-in-place? The shop becomes the upgrade UI.

Benefits of merging:
  • One system to maintain, not two
  • Clear progression path (Lv1 → Lv10) instead of confusing "basic vs enhanced vs advanced vs elite"
  • On-demand image generation works naturally (upgrade from Lv N to N+1)
  • Players understand "upgrade" better than "buy a whole new rover"
