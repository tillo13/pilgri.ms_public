The Signal — Phase 2 Spec

Synthesized from Luke's QA feedback on the original Signal brainstorm

April 2026 — Design decisions confirmed, open questions identified

Original Signal Brainstorm — Luke's comments from March 27-28, 2026

Detection & Finding — The Core Fix
Confirmed: Closest-approach detection. NOT destination-only bubble.
This is the single biggest change from the original design.

"Has to be closest approach. I dont like the 'destination only bubble' logic at all"

— Luke, Section 4

The original 42km detection radius only checked at the expedition destination. Phase 2 checks the entire path of each expedition — if your route passes within detection range of a signal at any point, you detect it.

Aspect Original Design Phase 2
Detection check Destination only Entire expedition path (closest approach)
Detection radius Fixed 42km for all Variable per signal (some easier, some harder)
What affects detection Discovery Rate only Crew stats + buildings + tech + Discovery Rate
Past expeditions Not checked Retroactive check when new code ships

Variable Detection Radius

"I like the idea some signals being easier to find (maybe bigger or smaller detection radius can have impact)"

— Luke, Section 3
Example Detection Radii

Hard signals (2-3 of 14)15-25km radius

Crew, Buildings & Tech as Detection Levers

"There should be more impact from Crew stats on finding signal. More functions from buildings and tech that increase this as well. Instead of current system that focuses mostly on increasing your 'Discovery Rate', that just makes Rares and Legendary more likely, this should incorporate Signal finding"

— Luke, Section 3

This means Depot building upgrades, Lab research, and crew stat progression all feed into Signal detection — not just item discovery rarity. Related bugs: "Scientist Stats do what?" (P1), "Recalibrate Depot building upgrades" (P1).

Retroactive Check

"I think we should do retroactive check based on new code, cuz we have been there, just wasnt part of game. Would be weird experience, for example if the signal you found popped up next time you went by a dot youd already visited 'wait, is that new? Why did it just pop up? Was it always there?'"

— Luke, Section 5
When the new detection code ships, all past expedition paths get re-evaluated. If a captain already passed within range, they get the detection retroactively — no confusion about "was this always here?"
ARIA Progression Triggers — Pacing the Reveal
Confirmed: Option B — slow reveal, don't front-load.
ARIA gives unprompted hints based on multiple triggers, not just time.

"I like Option B better. Gives ARIA more to do, more inclusive. I dont like when games try to jam everything into first couple days of games... can be overwhelming"

— Luke, Section 2

"Would suggest the progression is based on Sol#, plus # of expeditions. Like ARIA gives unprompted hints based on Sol Day, but also gives them based on # of returned expeditions, or maybe even certain building upgrades. Maybe even build in prompts based on ARIA stat progression (not currently built out in game)"

— Luke, Section 2
Trigger Type Example Threshold ARIA Behavior
Sol count Sol 30, 60, 90, 120 Increasingly specific hints about Signal existence
Returned expeditions 10, 25, 50, 100 Hints about what expeditions might find beyond resources
Building upgrades Depot L3, Lab L3, Scanner L2 "Your equipment is sensitive enough to detect... something"
ARIA stats Bond level milestones More personal, cryptic whispers about what she senses

Earlier Hints Needed

"Hard to say when i realized it... cuz i could say 2 weeks ago? More, and earlier hints, would def improve game play"

— Luke, Section 1

Luke didn't realize the Signal was a thing until ~Sol 150. Captains need breadcrumbs much earlier — not telling them what it is, but that something is out there.

Two-Step Claiming Flow — Detection is Not Discovery
Confirmed: Finding a signal (42km detection) is just step 1. You must send a separate expedition back to the site to claim it, with cinematic sequences.

"I think once you 'find' a signal (42km), you should then have to go to the site on separate expedition, with cinematic (maybe pre and post cinematic)"

— Luke, Section 5
Step 1: Detection
Expedition path passes within detection radius
Signal appears on your Signal page
ARIA whispers about what you sensed
→
Back at Colony
Review detection on Signal page
See approximate location on map
Plan your claiming expedition
→
Step 2: Claiming Expedition
Send expedition directly to signal site
Pre-cinematic on approach
Post-cinematic on discovery
Origin Site claimed

This creates a multi-day arc per signal: detect it during a normal expedition, come home, see it on your Signal page, plan a return trip, experience the cinematic. The discovery moment is earned, not accidental.

Open question: What happens during the claiming expedition? Is it a special expedition type? Does it cost more? Can it fail? Does the crew need certain stats?
Puzzle Fragments — Collectibles with ARIA Whispers

"I like the concept of puzzle fragments alot! More collecting always plus for me! Puzzle fragments should then always be accompanied by aria whisper"

— Luke, Section 6
Confirmed: Puzzle fragment collecting system. Every fragment comes with an ARIA whisper — a short, cryptic message that builds the Signal narrative piece by piece.

Fragments are found during expeditions (separate from Signal detection). They're breadcrumbs that build toward understanding the Signal. Each one triggers an ARIA whisper — a unique narrative moment that makes every fragment feel meaningful, not just a collectible counter.

Trading Between Captains

"Concept of Captain trading is interesting. Would depend on how 'easy' it was to get fragments. And if you didnt find other captains, are you severely penalized?"

— Luke, Section 6
Open design question: If fragment trading exists, solo captains must NOT be severely penalized. Two possible approaches:
  • Option A: All fragments findable solo. Trading just speeds it up.
  • Option B: No trading. Fragments are personal. Aligns with Luke's "self-contained path" philosophy.
Luke's multiplayer stance (next section) suggests Option B may be the better fit.
Multiplayer Philosophy — Passive, Not Competitive
Confirmed: The Signal is a self-contained journey for each captain. No competitive mechanics. No notifications. Passive evidence of others only.

"Interesting question about other captains... im leaning towards no notification, as that would assume all other captains knew each other and/or knew about signal. I also think the signal should be mostly a self contained path for each captain"

— Luke, Section 7

"I do acknowledge the cool concept of competing with other captains and the multiplayer experience, but i generally try to avoid this type of game play"

— Luke, Section 7

"I still think the tier system below works for this, as first captain will have name on it, thus signifying to later captains have found this item already"

— Luke, Section 7

The multiplayer element is passive: when you claim an Origin Site, you see that someone else was there first (their name on the tier). You don't race them, you don't get notified, you don't compete. You just see evidence of another captain's journey. This is the right balance — it feels like a shared world without the pressure of competitive mechanics.

Multiplayer Rules
Notifications when others find signalsNO
Race/competition mechanicsNO
First captain's name on tierYES (passive)
Self-contained path per captainYES
Captain-to-captain tradingTBD (see Puzzle Fragments)

"Yes, you should *something* for finding it. Even if its just SV/hr and/or Shard/hr generation. These new values MUST show up in Base homepage calculations. :)"

— Luke, Section 8
Hard requirement: Signal rewards MUST appear in the colony Base page income calculations. If a captain finds an Origin Site and their Shard/hr goes up, that increase must be visible in the main colony dashboard — not hidden in a separate Signal page.

World 2 — After All 14 Earth Signals

"My original thought was that finding all 14 earth objects, unlocks something new on MARS. Phase 2 being:
1. Maybe 14 more signals that have something to do with Martian heritage (build story/lore around that)
2. Excuse to revisit some dots you have already been to
3. Unlocks new technology branch
4. Maybe you even get a new Martian crew member?"

— Luke, Section 10/15

"After you find the 14 new Martian objects/signals, THEN it opens up the Jupiter or Zachalakia Planet stuff"

— Luke, Section 10/15
World 2 Progression:
  1. Phase 1 (current): Find 14 Earth Origin Sites
  2. Phase 2 — Martian Heritage: 14 new signals tied to Mars history/lore. Revisit already-explored dots. New tech branch unlocks. Possibly a new Martian crew member.
  3. Phase 3 — Beyond Mars: Jupiter, Zachalakia, or other planets open up

This is long-horizon vision, not immediate Phase 2 scope. But it confirms the Signal system has legs well beyond the initial 14 sites — and Luke's design gives revisiting old dots a reason, which is elegant.

Hold / Skeptical — Don't Build Yet

Luke flagged these as potentially diluting the Signal experience. They should NOT be built without further discussion.

Echo Sites

"Hmmmmm, need to be convinced on this one. Im a little worried this feature dilutes The Signal part of the game. Cant really say i dont enjoy more 'stuff' on signal page but... would need to see how busy that page is once we implement 'phase 2' of that page"

— Luke, Section 9

ARIA Bond Intersection

"Similar comment on this as Echo Sites... this is cool, but does it dilute the Captain ARIA Bond? Maybe this increases Captain/Scientist/ARIA stats instead?"

— Luke, Section 10

Blockchain / Decoder Terminal

"No real thoughts on any of this. I wont be trying to decode blockchains any time soon"

— Luke, Section 16

"I like puzzles though"

— Luke, Section 16
Takeaway: Keep the blockchain layer for players who want to dig deep, but don't make it required. Luke won't engage with it, but likes the puzzle framing. Echo Sites and ARIA Bond intersection are risky — revisit after the core Signal Phase 2 is built and the page isn't already too busy.
Implementation Priority — What to Build First

Based on Luke's feedback, ordered by impact and dependency:

Priority Item Why First
1 Closest-approach detection Everything else depends on signals actually being findable
2 Variable detection radius Pairs with #1, makes some signals achievable now
3 Retroactive check Rewards existing play, prevents confusion
4 Signal page UX overhaul Captains need to understand what they've found
5 Signal ↔ Map linking Usability gap in current product
6 ARIA progression triggers Gets players aware of Signal earlier
7 Two-step claiming flow Requires detection system working first
8 Puzzle fragments + ARIA whispers Narrative layer, can come after core mechanics
