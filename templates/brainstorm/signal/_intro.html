The Pitch — What IS This Game?
The game has to sound fun BEFORE you know about the endgame. Nobody plays Zelda because of the Triforce — they play because the world is fun to explore, the upgrades feel rewarding, and there's always something to do. The Triforce is what keeps them playing after 100 hours. Same here.

What the Game Actually Is (the daily fun)

"You're a captain on Mars. Build your base, upgrade your infrastructure, and send expeditions across an alien planet. Discover rare artifacts, level up your crew, unlock new vehicles, research alien tech, and grow your colony from a tiny outpost into a thriving operation. Your AI companion ARIA learns and grows alongside you. Real Mars terrain. Real weather. Real science. And somewhere out there... something ancient is waiting."
— The Full Game Pitch

That last sentence is the only mention of the endgame. Everything before it is what makes someone want to play today:

Then the Zelda Layer (the long game)

Players who stick around start noticing something. ARIA mentions patterns in the crystals. Expeditions bring back strange readings. The Signal page reveals hints of ancient sites hidden across Mars — real landing locations from Viking, Curiosity, Perseverance. 14 Origin Sites. Nobody's found most of them. Be the first.

This is the Triforce. You don't pitch it — players discover it. And once they know it exists, it's the reason they keep exploring every corner of Mars.

The Narrative Onramp — From "Mining Crystals" to "Hunting Signals"

The arrival lore tells players why they're on Mars:

"Probes found something in the soil. Crystals older than humanity. Energy that shouldn't exist. You're being sent to find out why."
— Arrival Screen

That's compelling — but it stops at "find out why." There's no thread connecting early gameplay to the Signal endgame. A new player builds their base, sends expeditions, collects discoveries... and never suspects that 14 ancient sites are hidden on Mars waiting to be found. The Signal exists as a separate system they might never discover.

The Gap: Players learn "crystals shouldn't exist" on arrival, then spend weeks mining/exploring with no hint that the crystals are breadcrumbs leading somewhere. The Signal page exists but nothing in the early game points to it. ARIA knows about the Origin Sites but never mentions them unprompted.

Should /signal Be Hidden or Visible?

Currently the Signal page is accessible but not linked from the main nav. Two options:

  • Option A — Always visible: Signal page in nav from day 1, but shows "No signal detected yet" for new players. Mystery from the start.
  • Option B — Unlocked by ARIA: Signal page appears in nav only after ARIA's Sol 20-30 hint. Creates a "new thing unlocked!" moment.
