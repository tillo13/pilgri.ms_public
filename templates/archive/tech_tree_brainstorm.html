We looked at how major strategy games structure their tech trees to inform our approach:

Game Total Techs Structure Key Mechanic
Civilization 6 ~118 (67 tech + 50 civics) 2 trees, 9 eras "Eureka" - mini-quests give 50% boost
Stellaris ~400 3 areas, 13 subcategories, 6 tiers Card shuffle randomizes available research
Factorio ~200 Multiple paths, progressive Science packs as currency, infinite late-game
Age of Empires IV ~50-70 per civ By building type, 4 ages Civilization-specific trees
Dawn of Man ~30-40 Adaptive unlock Knowledge from actions (hunt, build, craft)
Key Design Principles We Found:
  • Branching factor ~1.5 - Civ V uses 1.49. Too many branches = decision paralysis.
  • "One choice = no choice" - Every tech should feel useful, not just a prerequisite.
  • Thematic pillars - Organize around 3-5 themes (for us: Exploration, Vehicles, Power, Extraction).
  • Dawn of Man insight - "You get knowledge for doing things" = Luke's idea of science points from extraction!

Our scale recommendation: For an indie game our size, 20-30 techs is manageable. Civ's 118 or Stellaris's 400 would be overkill.

Sources: Civ Wiki • Stellaris Wiki • Factorio Wiki • Dawn of Man GDC • CivFanatics Design Guide

Christian's Feedback: Smarter Scientist Bonuses
"If Dr. Andy is an astrophysicist, maybe he's in one of the branches with a higher level skill. Like out of the gate he has a bonus to exploration, but when you get into higher tiers (like vehicle AI navigation) he would have a bonus too."
— Christian's Feedback

The Refinement: Instead of "one branch = flat bonus", scientists could have cross-branch bonuses at different tiers:

Example: Dr. Andy (Astrobiology)
Tier 1 Exploration +25% (primary)
Tier 2 Vehicles → AI Navigation +15% (cross-branch)
Tier 3 Extraction +10% (minor)

This creates more interesting scientist choices - some are specialists (one branch, big bonus), others are generalists (multiple branches, smaller bonuses). Matches the WoW frustration: "My priest should get a bonus to healing potions because that's what priests do."

Christian's Feedback: Echo Shards as Real Items
"In World of Warcraft you have memories that give you experience but they don't actually DO anything for you as a character. It would be really cool to have echo shards and be able to learn an older variation of a spell or skill... Plus you can introduce lore as part of it. Even for quests."
— Christian's Feedback

The Expansion: Echo Shards shouldn't just swap scientists - they should be collectible items with real gameplay value:

💠 Echo Shard Uses
Learn Ancient Skills
Absorb an echo to unlock an older variation of an ability (e.g., "Ancient Extraction" - slower but +30% yield)
Unlock Crafting Patterns
Echoes contain blueprints for unique equipment variants (cosmetic or functional)
Lore Fragments
Each echo reveals a piece of Mars history - collectible story content
Quest Triggers
Certain echoes unlock special expeditions or story missions

This transforms echoes from "scientist swap mechanic" into a core discovery system that ties into lore, skills, and quests. Found during expeditions, each one is meaningful.

Open Question: Swapping Scientists

Luke wants ability to swap out Dr. Bo. Options we discussed:

Option A: Shard Transmutation

Same concept as commander transmutation. Pay escalating shards (50k → 100k → 200k) to transform your scientist into a different one. "The shards reshape reality."

Option B: Shard Echo (More Lore-Aligned)

Find "Shard Echoes" during expeditions - imprints of past scientists stored in crystals. Absorbing an echo changes your scientist's specialty. Same person, new expertise. "The shards remember everyone who touched them."

Option C: Recruit via Expeditions

Find cryopods from failed missions. Recruit new scientists you discover. Can swap between ones you've found.

Option D: Specialty Evolution

Dr. Bo stays Dr. Bo, but through research they can LEARN a new specialty. Tech tree unlock enables this.

🚨 Expedition Speed Problem (CRITICAL)
"My Hesperia expedition has 639 hours remaining... that's 26+ days!"
— Current state of distant expeditions

The Problem: Current expeditions can take 600+ hours for distant locations. While realistic for Mars distances, this may frustrate players who want to see progress.

💡 Solution Ideas
A) Scientist Research
Tech tree unlocks like "Shard-Infused Propulsion: -20% travel time"
B) Captain Stats
Logistics/Navigation stat affects expedition speed (+1% per point)
C) Shard Acceleration
Spend 10,000 shards to boost active expedition by -10% remaining time
Limit: Max 3 boosts per expedition (creates meaningful choice)
D) Vehicle Upgrades
Make existing Rover/Drone level speed bonuses more impactful
E) Lore Angle
"The crystals hum at frequencies that resonate with Martian terrain..."
Shards contain ancient propulsion knowledge. Scientists study them to unlock faster travel.

Current math: 1 km ≈ 0.5 hours. 1,500 km = 750 hours (31 days).

Target: With full upgrades, reduce to ~0.3 hours/km. Max expedition ~7-10 days instead of 26+.

💬 Scientist Quotes - Untapped Potential
"We see these everywhere. Standard extraction procedure—307 shards, nothing special."
— Current HARDCODED quote for ALL common/uncommon items

The Problem: Scientist specialty is completely wasted. A Geologist says the same thing about minerals as a Biologist does. Every scientist has the same generic quote.

🔬 Make Quotes Dynamic
A) Specialty-Based Quotes
Geologist: "Fascinating crystalline structure!" vs Biologist: "No organic compounds detected."
B) Tech Tree Unlocks
As scientists research items, they gain deeper knowledge and better quotes
C) Matching Bonus
Scientist analyzing matching item type gives bonus SP or extra shards
D) Progressive Learning
1st time: "Interesting specimen." 10th time: "Based on our research, I can extract 15% more efficiently now."

MVP: Just vary quotes by scientist specialty. Later: unlock better quotes/bonuses via tech tree.

Connects to: Tech tree research, extraction bonuses, personalized gameplay experience.
