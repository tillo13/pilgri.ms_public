About Pilgrims

Why This Exists

Honestly? Frustration.

Sitting around one night, listing games we'd loved—XCOM, Civilization, Factorio, BattleTech. Hundreds of hours in each. But the same problem kept coming up: we're adults now. Jobs. Kids. The games we grew up with demanded hours we don't have. And the new "mobile games" feel like they're designed by casino psychologists.

Everything wants your attention. Everything punishes you for having a life. Miss a day? Your streak is broken. Gone for a week? Your clan kicked you. Your crops died. Your base got raided.

The TV Show You Never Have to Watch

What if a game worked more like a TV show? Episodes happen whether you're watching or not. Miss a few? The story continues. Binge when you want. Skip months if you want. Nobody guilt-trips you for having other things going on.

Your Mars colony runs itself. An AI—think AUTO from WALL-E, but actually helpful, not nefarious—keeps things going. You can dive deep and optimize everything, or you can get a "here's what happened" email once a month. Both are fine. Neither is wrong.

For the Screen-Time Argument

If you have kids, you know the screen fights. "Five more minutes." "I can't stop now." Apps are engineered to be sticky. That's the business model.

We wanted the opposite. Progress without presence. A game that doesn't demand your eyes. Check in on your colony like you'd check in on a friend—when you feel like it, because you're curious, not because you're afraid something bad will happen if you don't.

Real Data, Because Why Not

The locations are real. Olympus Mons, Valles Marineris, Jezero Crater—coordinates from NASA's databases. Decades of orbital surveys. When your captain lands somewhere on Mars, that place actually exists.

It doesn't add anything to the gameplay, really. It just felt right.

We're Not Selling Anything

No ads. No premium currency. No loot boxes. No "limited time offers." The Sepolia shards you collect are tracked permanently using some nerdy tech we won't bore you with, but there's no credit card field anywhere.

This isn't a startup. There's no investor deck. It's just a game two people wanted to play that didn't exist, so we made it.

The Bottom Line

Your colony will be here. Mars isn't going anywhere.
Play or don't. Your pace. Your pilgrimage.
