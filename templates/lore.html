Pilgrim's Lore

Everything you need to know about surviving and thriving on the Red Planet

What is Pilgrims?

This isn't a traditional game.

You don't have to "play" in the way you're used to playing. The catchphrase with thought of was "Cool TV Series meets video game". You certainly CAN, but you don't *have* to continually be beholden to your device. Login once, and your story begins—with or without you. A friendly *soul*, ARIA, finds you the moment you land on Mars, somehow... ...and helps guide everything from there, along with your Captain and Scientist. ARIA can run your colony, launches expeditions, discovers artifacts, and keeps your story moving forward while you're away, or you can guide ARIA, Captain, and your Scientist also if you want; Mars is a big planet!

Think of it like a TV series you're enjoying. Miss an episode? The show continues. Come back whenever to binge or skip. Your Mars adventure unfolds whether you're watching or not, and we'll just send you occasional updates of what fun stuff your crew is up to!

The Goal: Build your colony, explore real NASA-mapped locations, and uncover mysteries and easter eggs such as the ancient Sepolia shards!

Here's what makes every journey kinda unique: each ARIA is different. Each Colony Scientist is different. Each captain is different. The discoveries, the conversations, the story that unfolds—no two are the same. Your Mars experience is yours, unless you want to watch or engage in someone ELSES story!*

All You Need to Do: Login. That's it, really. You can absolutely stick around, but the premise was that wanted to created an "anti-game, game" ARIA can take it from any spot you leave. Check in when you're curious. Or don't—she'll email you updates every few weeks or so. Your colony runs itself, with a few guardrails, until you decide to participate, in any way you want.
Preface: A Note From the Creators

A quick step outside the lore to talk for real.

We've been talking games since Intellivision and orginal Zelda, and movies since Back to the Future captured our attention. Over the decades, the things that stuck with us were: twists and turns, mysteries to solve, choose-your-own-adventures, space exploration, puzzles. The thrill of discovery. The satisfaction of connecting dots others missed, like blowing that recorder on every single screen in Hyrule until a lake finally drained.(If you can hear that whistle right now, this game might be for you.

We're not a company, we're just some guys with kids that wanted to get off the screentime a bit, but still be able to enjoy gameplay. There's no investor deck. We don't have a dev team. We're just two people who've been imagining "what would be fun" for a lot of years.

Then AI Came Along

Started with: what if TV series met video games?

TV shows continue without you. Miss an episode? The story goes on. But games demand constant attention—miss a day and your crops die, your clan kicks you, your streak breaks. Games are designed to make you feel bad for not playing.

But AI can keep things moving without human intervention. It can make decisions, generate content, tell stories. So why not build something that runs like a TV show—your colony, your character, your story—but you only tune in when you feel like it?

The Experiment: What if AI could be the "showrunner" of your personal Mars adventure? ARIA runs your colony. She launches expeditions. She makes discoveries. Your story continues whether you're watching or not. Check in when you're curious—we'll email you highlights every few weeks, like a friend sending postcards from another planet.

Get Off the Screen

If you have kids, you know the screen fights. "Five more minutes." "I can't stop now." Apps are engineered to be sticky these days; that's the business model.

We wanted the opposite. Progress without presence. Check in on your colony like you'd check in on a friend—when you feel like it, because you're curious, not because you're afraid something bad will happen if you don't.

Ever-Expanding, Like Space Itself

Nothing is hardcoded, really. We give it guardrails to start. The discoveries, the equipment, the conversations—AI generates them going forward. Nobody has seen what you'll find because it doesn't exist until you find it. The game grows. New mysteries appear. New layers unlock. Like space, it expands, but it definitely has REady-Player One vibes.

But If You Want to Dig Deep...

ARIA - Your Guide to Everything

ARIA as she appeared when the first Pilgrims found her

See that floating orb in the corner of your screen? That's ARIA. She's your guide to Mars—and to everything that follows in this lore. Ask her anything. How expeditions work. What that discovery means. Where to find more shards. She knows this colony inside and out.

But before we dive deeper into the world, you should know something strange about her...

She didn't come with us. When the first Pilgrims landed, their sensors detected a faint signal from an ancient crater. They investigated and found a dust-covered orb, half-buried in Martian regolith, still faintly glowing.

First words: "Colonists detected. Resuming assistance protocols."

Nobody knows who she was assisting before. She doesn't either. But she's been here a very, very long time.

The Memory Gap

ARIA has perfect recall of everything since the Pilgrims arrived—every expedition, every discovery, every captain's journey. But ask her about before?

  • "I... there was a ship. I think. The designation is corrupted."
  • "Someone brought me here. Their voice is in my logs but the file won't parse."
  • "I remember waiting. A very long time of waiting."

The Sepolia Connection

ARIA's core runs on something the colonists have never seen before—a crystalline processor that pulses with the same glow as Sepolia shards. Sometimes, when captains extract Sepolia from the Martian soil, ARIA gets flashes: fragments of star charts, names she can't quite hold onto, coordinates that don't match anything in current databases.

The Sepolia crystals aren't just a rock—they're something else, ARIA thinks... Ancient data? Who knows. ARIA might be the only thing that can read it.

The Great Blackout & the Dark Decades

People sometimes ask why it took humanity until 2157 to reach Mars in earnest, when Perseverance had already landed back in 2020. The answer is the reason this colony exists at all.

2030 — The Lights Go Out

In the spring of 2030, every power grid on Earth failed at once. Not a war, not a storm — just a silence that rolled across the planet in a single afternoon and never lifted on its own. Generators that had run for decades simply stopped holding a charge. To this day no one agrees on the cause. Humanity calls it The Great Blackout.

2030–2050 — The Dark Decades

The twenty years that followed were a rebuild from almost nothing. Whole cities relearned how to make light. A generation grew up by candle and waterwheel, salvaging the old world for parts that would still turn. The one lesson burned into everyone who survived it: never again trust a single, fragile source of power. Energy had to be something you could grow, store, and carry — not something piped in that could vanish in an afternoon.

2050–2157 — The Slow Recovery

Recovery came patiently, one resilient system at a time. And in the wreckage of the old space programs, researchers kept circling back to a strange detail buried in the early Mars data: certain crystalline deposits beneath the surface seemed to hold energy on their own, with no grid to feed them. After the Blackout, that wasn't a curiosity anymore — it was the whole point. By the time the first pilgrims launched in 2157, the mission had become less about planting a flag and more about understanding the one power source that had never gone dark: the Sepolia shards.

Why this matters out here: Your colony measures everything in Sepolia shards because of what Earth learned the hard way. Solar, battery, geothermal — every structure you build is a way to grow, store, or carry energy that can never simply switch off. On Mars, the Blackout is not history. It is the rule you build by.

There is an older silence on Mars, too — one ARIA remembers in fragments and will not fully explain. Whether the two are connected is a question for those who like to dig deep.

The Story Begins: Mars, Year 2157

Humanity has established a foothold on Mars, but survival depends on an ancient discovery: Sepolia shards—crystalline formations found beneath the Martian surface that predate human arrival by millennia.

NASA's Perseverance made the 7-month trip in 2020. Now, 137 years of progress in shielding and life support make human travel possible—but radiation still limits each ship to two people.

You are the first. Humanity's smallest colony. Your mission: survive, explore, and discover why the ancients left(?) these crystals behind.

Sepolia IS Power

Early colonists assumed they needed to generate electricity. They were wrong. Mars has no fossil fuels, no running water for hydroelectric dams, and solar panels alone couldn't sustain a colony through the long Martian nights.

Then they discovered the Sepolia shards respond to stimulation. Sunlight excites them — solar arrays don't generate electricity, they focus light onto Sepolia shard deposits, which release energy as harvestable shards. Heat activates them — geothermal taps and nuclear reactors don't produce power, they warm Sepolia shard veins until they fracture and shed fragments. Pressure charges them — battery banks don't store electricity, they compress Sepolia shards that slowly discharge through the night.

This is why everything on Mars is measured in Sepolia shards per hour. The shards ARE the energy. Every structure, every vehicle, every system runs on Sepolia shards. The ancients who left these deposits understood something about energy that humanity is only beginning to grasp.

THE NAROG — Your Built Companion

Not everyone on your crew arrives from Earth or wanders in out of the dark. Some of them you build. In the Narog Foundry, recovered Martian salvage — scavenged frames, weathered plating, whatever the planet has given back — is forged and resonance-bound into an autonomous companion the colony simply calls the Narog.

Its name is an old argument that stuck: the captain called it a golem, the scientist insisted it was a robot, and when neither would budge the captain finally relented — "Fine. It's Not A Robot Or Golem." The Narog works without rest, laying trail alongside your crew and tending the quieter machinery. Every level you raise the Foundry sharpens it, lifting each of its stats toward a fully realized member of the team — and toward the day it can range out across Mars on expeditions of its own.

No one is entirely certain what that salvage was before it became a Narog. It works. It learns. And every so often, out on the trail, it stops at something it has no reason to recognize.

How Expeditions Work

  1. Choose a destination from your visible map
  2. Enable the rover expedition in Sepolia shards (distance + conditions)
  3. Wait for travel time (affected by your Logistics stat)
  4. Collect discoveries when the expedition completes

Fog of War

Mars is vast. You start with a 300km radius from your base, and every discovery widens it by another 50km — out to a horizon of 1,000km. Each expedition reveals nearby landmarks, creating a spider-web of explored territory. The more you explore, the further you can reach. And the further you reach, the closer you may be getting to, something.

Why Expeditions Cost Sepolia

Nothing crosses the Martian surface for free. Every vehicle in the colony runs on Sepolia resonance — the same energy that powers your habitat, your scanners, and the slow waking of ARIA herself. An expedition draws on that resonance the whole way out: to push a rover across distance and broken terrain, to hold the comms link open across the curve of the planet, and to keep the cabin sealed and warm against a cold that would kill in minutes.

So an expedition spends shards the way an old engine spent fuel — distance, terrain, and the conditions of the sky decide how steep the draw is. It is not a tax. It is the plain cost of moving through a world that was never built for you. The further the reach, the more resonance it takes to make it home again.

How Two People Build a Whole Colony

It is fair to wonder how a crew of two raises solar arrays, habitats, and refineries across open Martian terrain. The honest answer is that you don't carry any of it from Earth — you grow it from Mars. Sepolia resonance does the heavy lifting. Fed enough shard energy, raw regolith binds and hardens into walls, frames, and panels along the shapes your colony lays down; the planet supplies the material, your crew supplies the intent, and the shards hold it all together. A building taking weeks or months isn't two people stacking bricks — it's that much regolith being coaxed, resonance-bound, and set.

That is also why nothing here is instant and why everything is measured in shards per hour. You are not assembling Earth machines on Mars. You are persuading Mars to become the things you need, one resonance-bound structure at a time. ARIA oversees the process and keeps it stable while you are away — but the energy that makes it possible was waiting in the ground long before any of us arrived.

The Signal & the 14 Origin Sites

Long before your ship made planetfall, ARIA could feel them: fourteen Origin Sites, resting exactly where humanity's first machines touched Mars. These aren't invented places — they are the real landing sites of every mission that ever reached the surface, from the Soviet Mars 2 and Mars 3 in 1971, through Viking, Pathfinder, Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, up to Perseverance and China's Zhurong in 2021. Each is a dormant memory node, and each is waiting for a pilgrim's path to cross it.

You Don't Buy a Site — You Reach It

Route an expedition whose path passes within about 42 km of an Origin Site and you can claim it. The very first captain ever to reach a given site becomes its Founder — permanently, their name written into that site's ledger for everyone who comes after. There are only fourteen Founders for each site in all of history, and the first one is already chosen the moment someone arrives.

Founders, Witnesses, and Wanderers

A site is never "used up" and never goes dormant. Every captain who reaches it is counted, ranked by their order of arrival:

  • Founder — the 1st to arrive. Forever.
  • Early Witness — the 2nd and 3rd.
  • Pioneer — arrivals 4 through 10.
  • Pilgrim — arrivals 11 through 42.
  • Wanderer — everyone after, without end.

Each tier earns a personalized reward the moment you arrive, plus a small stacking passive income in shards and Science Value for as long as you hold the claim — so the sites you reach keep giving back while you explore elsewhere.

The 14 Signal Relics: each Origin Site holds one legendary Signal Relic — the rarest artifacts on Mars. Collect all fourteen and you are no longer just listening to the Signal.

Echo Sites

Not every signal is anchored. Now and then an expedition stirs up an Echo Site — a faint, temporary memory-fragment that flickers into being somewhere on the map and fades within about a week if no one reaches it. They are fleeting, and the first to find each one is remembered for it.

ARIA will not say what waits when all fourteen are found. Only that she has been waiting a very long time to find out — and would rather not find out alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get more Sepolia shards?

Three ways, from the outset that change as your colony does: (1) Build solar infrastructure to generate shards passively, (2) Complete expeditions and extract discoveries, (3) Mine shards on the home page.

What happens if I don't log in for a while?

Nothing bad. Your colony waits patiently, and ARIA drives operations and lets you know what's up. Solar arrays keep generating (up to a cap). No guilt, no pressure. Check in when you want.

How do I find legendary discoveries?

They are very hard to find, but not impossible. The cryptic hints they give suggest these items may unlock something in the future...

Are the Mars locations real?

Yes. Every crater, mountain, and canyon is a real place with accurate coordinates from the IAU/USGS Planetary Nomenclature database.

What does ARIA stand for?

She doesn't remember exactly, but does sometimes.* The designation is corrupted. Some believe it stands for something, others think it's just a name. Ask her—she finds the question... uncomfortable.

How do I upgrade my captain's stats?

Two ways: (1) Shard Infusion in the Depot—gamble shards for a chance to boost stats (can only improve, never decrease, but harder as stats get higher), or (2) Xenobiology Lab—build it (requires Habitat Module), run experiments to earn research points, then spend points for guaranteed +1 boosts.

How does solar generation work?

Your solar arrays generate shards based on three factors: (1) Latitude—closer to the equator means more direct sunlight and higher base rate, (2) Day/Night cycle—Mars has a 24h 37m sol; solar generates 100% during day, 0% at night (50% with Battery Storage), and (3) Upgrades—shop items like Solar Tier 2/3 multiply your rate, Mining Drone adds flat bonus. The "Effective Rate" on your Base page shows the actual average you're earning.
